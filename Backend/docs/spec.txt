Note on terminology
Section 2.1 addresses households in which daughters are not permitted to travel outside the home for education. This document describes that circumstance in neutral descriptive language — "restricted household mobility" — rather than characterising the families themselves. The wording is chosen deliberately: the specification is an engineering document, the constraint is a design input, and neutral framing keeps the analysis defensible in academic review while describing exactly the same real situation.

1.1 Executive summary
Ustaad.com is a verified tutor discovery and matching platform for Pakistan. It addresses a market that is large, entirely informal, and coordinated almost exclusively through WhatsApp groups, notice boards and word of mouth — with no mechanism for verifying that a tutor can teach what they claim to teach, no way to compare prices quoted in incompatible units, and no record of what was agreed or paid.
The platform contributes four things that existing tutor directories do not. First, it serves households where a female student cannot travel to a tuition centre, by making verified female home tutors discoverable under a hard gender constraint that the system enforces rather than merely suggests. Second, it replaces self-declared expertise with assessed expertise: every subject claim is challenged by an AI examiner at topic level, and every identity is verified by the platform administrator against a CNIC and academic documents before the profile is approved. Third, it matches at topic level rather than subject level, using an AI diagnostic agent that interviews a parent to locate the actual gap — which is frequently two chapters upstream of the symptom. Fourth, it makes the market legible: rates are normalised to a comparable basis, benchmarked against local medians, and every engagement records what was agreed and what was paid.
The platform also supports engagement shapes the market currently cannot express — a single hourly session for concept clarification or assessment support, a structured trial before any monthly commitment, and group tuition pooled across neighbouring students at a reduced per-head rate.


2. Problem Statement
Private tuition in Pakistan is a large, entirely informal market. Coordination happens through WhatsApp groups, notice boards, and personal referral. Seven distinct failures are consistent across the market, and the first is the most consequential because it determines whether education continues at all.
2.1 Restricted household mobility denies girls access to tuition entirely
In a significant proportion of Pakistani households — particularly in conservative communities, rural districts, and lower-income urban neighbourhoods — daughters are not permitted to travel outside the home for education. Tuition centres, academies and a male tutor's residence are all unavailable to them. Where the family will not permit travel, the practical outcome is that supplementary education stops, regardless of the student's ability or the family's willingness to pay.
For these families a single arrangement remains acceptable: a female tutor who comes to the home, teaches the student there, and additionally provides the personal mentoring and grooming that families expect alongside academic instruction. This arrangement is not a preference to be accommodated at the margins. It is the only viable channel, and for a large group of students it is the difference between continued schooling support and none.
The informal market serves this need badly. Finding a female tutor who is genuinely qualified, will travel to a specific area, and can be trusted in the family home is done entirely by referral. There is no verification, no record, and no recourse. Families either accept an unverified stranger on a neighbour's recommendation, or they do without.
Design consequence
Gender is treated as a hard, system-enforced exclusion criterion rather than a ranking preference (§6.16). A dedicated home-tuition pathway combines a female-only constraint, home-delivery mode, area matching and guardian-presence expectations into a single flow (§6.29). Because the tutor is a woman travelling to an unfamiliar residence, the same module provides reciprocal safety controls for her — she may restrict herself to female students, require guardian presence, and see the area before accepting any engagement. Safety in this arrangement is two-directional, and the specification treats it as such.
2.2 Expertise is asserted, never demonstrated
Anyone can list themselves as an FSc Physics tutor. No existing platform in this market tests the claim. A parent discovers whether a tutor actually commands the syllabus only after paying for a month of sessions, by which point both the money and the student's time are gone. The parent has no instrument for assessing subject competence, which is precisely why referral persists — a neighbour's experience is weak evidence, but it is the only evidence available.
2.3 No verification, no accountability, and consequently fraud
The informal market has no identity verification whatsoever. A tutor may misrepresent qualifications, experience, or identity with no possibility of detection and no consequence. Fabricated degrees, inflated experience, and in the worst cases impersonation are all unchecked. Because no institution stands behind any arrangement, a family that is defrauded — of fees, of a student's exam term, or of trust — has nowhere to take the complaint.
Payment compounds this. Rates are agreed verbally. Nothing records what was agreed, what was delivered, or what was paid. Disputes over fees for sessions that did not take place, or sessions delivered but not paid for, are resolved by whoever argues more forcefully, and both tutors and families lose in this arrangement.
Ustaad.com addresses this through platform-owned verification. Every tutor is verified by the platform administrator against a CNIC and original academic documents before the profile becomes approved and searchable. The verification is performed by the platform, recorded by the platform, timestamped, attributed to the approving administrator, and displayed publicly as a specific statement of what was checked. Ustaad.com owns the verification chain; it is not delegated to the tutor's own assertion or to a third party. Every engagement additionally records the agreed rate and the payment status acknowledged by both parties, producing an auditable trail where the informal market produces nothing.
An important boundary, stated plainly
Ustaad.com records and makes transparent what was agreed and what both parties confirm was paid. It does not process payments, hold funds, or move money. Payment settlement remains directly between the family and the tutor. This distinction is deliberate and is stated in these terms wherever payment is discussed (§6.31, §4.2). Building a payment gateway would require licensed payment-service integration, know-your-customer obligations, and a funded dispute-resolution process — none of which is achievable within this project, and claiming otherwise would misrepresent the system. Transparency of record is achievable, is genuinely valuable against fraud, and is what the platform delivers.
2.4 Matching happens at the wrong granularity
Existing directories match at subject level: a parent searches for a "Maths tutor". The actual problem is almost always at topic level and is almost always upstream of the symptom. A student failing quadratic equations usually has an unresolved weakness in signed-number arithmetic or algebraic factorisation, two chapters earlier. A tutor engaged to teach quadratics will teach quadratics, and the student will continue to fail, because the prerequisite was never repaired.
Subject-level search cannot express this problem, and parents are not equipped to diagnose it — that is a teacher's skill. So families hire on proximity, price and gender, and hope.
2.5 Feedback carries no usable signal
Star ratings compress to a meaningless average. On every platform in this category, effectively every listed tutor sits near 4.7 stars, which distinguishes nothing. The real signal exists in review text — whether the tutor arrives on time, whether they teach the concept or simply complete the homework, whether they know the board's paper pattern, whether the student's confidence changed — but that text is unstructured, uncomparable, and unweighted by whether the reviewer had one session or thirty.
2.6 The market has no short engagement, and no way to try before committing
The default unit in Pakistani tuition is one month, frequently paid in advance. This produces two failures at once.
A family that discovers a mismatch — wrong pace, wrong communication style, wrong subject depth — discovers it four weeks and twelve thousand rupees late. There is no trial mechanism.
Separately, and just as importantly, the market cannot serve short needs at all. A student who needs one hour on a single misunderstood concept, one session to work through a returned assessment paper, or a focused revision block before a paper has no way to buy that. The available product is a month. Because the minimum purchase is far larger than the actual need, the need goes unmet — the student either struggles on alone or the family commits to a month they did not want. A market whose smallest unit is thirty times the smallest need is failing a large volume of genuine demand.
Design consequence
Ustaad.com supports three engagement types as first-class concepts: monthly ongoing, short-term package, and single session (§6.30). A single session may be booked on an hourly basis for a declared purpose — concept clarification, assessment or paper review, doubt-solving, or pre-examination revision — with no monthly commitment and no obligation on either side to continue. This is paired with a structured trial mechanism (§6.20) so that a family entering an ongoing arrangement does so after evidence rather than before it.
2.7 Prices are quoted in incompatible units
Rates are quoted monthly, hourly, and per-session interchangeably. "Rupees eight thousand a month for three days a week" and "rupees nine hundred an hour" cannot be compared by inspection, and no one performs the arithmetic. Neither families nor tutors know the prevailing rate for their subject, level and area. Tutors under-price themselves in ignorance; families over-pay in ignorance; and no one can tell which is happening.
2.8 No accountability for outcomes
A family paying fifteen thousand rupees a month for six months has no evidence of progress beyond the tutor's own reassurance. Nothing records which topics were covered, whether mastery improved, or whether the original weakness was ever addressed. The arrangement is expensive, long-running, and entirely unmeasured.

5. Stakeholders, Roles and Permissions
5.1 Role definitions
Role
Definition
ADMIN
Platform operator acting on behalf of Ustaad.com. Verifies tutor identity against CNIC and academic documents, approves or rejects profiles, manages taxonomy, resolves flags and verification appeals, and reviews payment disputes. Every administrator action is logged immutably
TUTOR
Individual offering tuition. Owns profile, documents, topic claims, availability, pricing, engagement-type offerings, group willingness, and personal safety constraints
PARENT
Account holder acting for one or more minor students. The sole contact channel for any minor on the platform
STUDENT
Self-managing learner aged 18 or over. Holds the same capabilities as a Parent, exercised on their own behalf
ORGANIZATION
Academy, school or tuition centre searching for tutors and posting vacancies

Design note — why Parent and Student are separate roles
Under-18 learners hold no account on Ustaad.com. They exist as student profiles owned by a Parent account. All discovery, booking, communication, payment acknowledgement and review activity is performed by the parent. This structurally prevents any private channel between an adult tutor and a minor from existing in the system at all — it is not a policy that could be violated, it is an absence in the data model. This is a core safety property of the platform, not an implementation convenience, and it holds within group tuition as well as one-to-one engagements.

Rationale — why monthly is primary and why single-session pricing is separate
Pakistani tuition is contracted monthly: "eight thousand rupees a month, three days a week." A model that accepts only hourly rates forces tutors to enter figures they did not agree to and do not use, which corrupts the data at source. Normalisation (FR-5.8) is therefore computed rather than entered, which gives families the cross-offer comparability the informal market cannot provide. FR-5.7 exists because a one-off hour genuinely costs more per hour than a committed month — travel and preparation are not amortised — and forcing a single rate would either overprice monthly engagements or make single sessions uneconomic for the tutor.

This is a small clause with a real effect, and it exists because the specification treats the visiting tutor as a person at risk rather than as supply to be optimised.

6.31 Payment transparency and fraud prevention
This module implements the transparency requirement in §2.3. Its boundary is stated first, because the boundary is the most important thing about it.
Scope boundary — stated before the requirements
Ustaad.com records what was agreed and what both parties confirm was paid. It does not process payments, hold funds, act as an escrow, or move money in any form. Settlement remains directly between the family and the tutor. Every requirement below is a record-keeping and transparency requirement. This boundary is repeated in the project README and in the application interface itself, so that no user can form the impression that the platform guarantees or handles their money.

FR-31.12 is deliberate: allowing payment history to affect public ranking would create an incentive for tutors to pressure families over acknowledgements, which would corrupt the record the module exists to protect.
6.32 Platform feedback and product improvement
This module is distinct from §6.9. 6.33 Volunteer tutor programme
Not every qualified person teaches for a fee. University students, retired teachers and working professionals will give a limited number of hours without charge, and the informal market has no way to find them, no way to verify them, and no way to hold them to any standard. A formal volunteer channel serves two purposes: it reaches students whose families cannot pay at any rate, and it directs willing supply at the restricted-mobility pathway in §6.29, where verified female home tutors are scarcest. A volunteer is verified on exactly the same basis as a paid tutor under §6.6. The fee is what differs, not the standard.

Why the database write and the mail dispatch are both required, in that order
EmailJS dispatches mail from the browser using a public key. It is free, it needs no mail server, and it is the right tool for notifying a small team. It is not a system of record. A monthly quota reached, a template renamed, or a blocked request would silently discard an application if the email were the only artefact — and the applicant would never know, because from the browser the submission appeared to succeed.
FR-33.9 therefore fixes the order: the row is written first and the email is a notification of a record that already exists. FR-33.8 states the security position plainly — a public key is designed to be public, but it is still an open send endpoint, so rate limiting and a honeypot are requirements rather than refinements. FR-33.10 is the load-bearing rule of the module: goodwill is not a substitute for verification, and a volunteer entering a family home is verified exactly as a paid tutor is.

7.2 Architectural principle
The model classifies, narrates and sequences. The application code computes, validates and enforces.
Every numeric or binding output visible to a user — tutor ranking, per-topic scores, normalised hourly rates, credibility weights, rate benchmarks, reliability statistics, group compositions, session counts, dates, and every hard constraint — is produced by deterministic functions operating on stored structured signals. The model never emits a score, a price, a ranking, a date, or a constraint decision.

The enforcement points are specific and testable:
Requirement
Enforcement mechanism
FR-10.12, FR-16.4, FR-29.2
Hard constraints — gender, budget, area, engagement type — filtered in application code after the tool call, never delegated to model judgment
FR-22.4
Narration prompt forbidden from introducing any figure not present in the supplied breakdown
FR-26.2
Prerequisite ordering validated in code after generation; the plan is regenerated on violation
FR-26.4
All session-count and date arithmetic performed in code
FR-23.7
Group matching is constraint satisfaction, not model judgment
FR-7.5, FR-17.7, FR-19.6, FR-24.7
Ranking, reliability statistics, rate benchmarks and demand aggregation are all deterministic

The consequences are that rankings are reproducible across runs, auditable against stored signals, explainable to a user, and unaffected by model non-determinism or by substitution of one provider for another. Where a decision could affect a family's safety or a tutor's livelihood, that decision is made by code whose behaviour can be inspected and tested.
7.3 Prompt management
Prompts are versioned Markdown files held under a prompts directory and loaded at runtime rather than embedded in source code:
/prompts
    diagnostic-intake.v1.md
    competency-verification.v1.md
    review-intelligence.v1.md
    ranking-explanation.v1.md
    study-plan.v1.md
Every AI output row persists the model identifier and the prompt version that produced it. This gives per-record provenance, allows a prompt revision to be evaluated against prior output, and makes the README's disclosure of the prompt instructions verifiable rather than merely asserted.
7.4 Cost-control design
The platform is designed to operate within a permanent free inference tier. This is an architectural constraint, addressed architecturally.

Data-handling disclosure — required README content
Free-tier inference providers may use submitted prompts to improve their models. All demonstration data on Ustaad.com is synthetic. A production deployment would require a paid or self-hosted inference tier, because the platform processes information concerning minors and, in the restricted-mobility pathway, information about which households a female tutor visits. This constraint is documented rather than ignored, and the mitigation — that no real user data is submitted during this project — is stated explicitly.

10.2 Database decision — SQLite now, Supabase on deployment
The requirement is a database that is free, does not delay the start of development, and persists across redeployments and serverless cold starts once the application is live. Those halves of the requirement are best served by two different things, so the decision is staged rather than single: development runs against a local SQLite file, and the schema is migrated to a hosted Supabase Postgres instance at the point of deployment to Netlify.
Why a local SQLite file cannot be used in production
Serverless hosting platforms provide an ephemeral filesystem. A .db file committed to the repository becomes a read-only build artefact: write operations either fail outright or land in a temporary directory that is discarded on the next request, and every redeployment resets the database to whatever was last committed. A tutor could register, and the record would be gone within minutes.
There is a second and more serious objection. If writes did persist into the repository, the platform would be committing user data — phone numbers, CNIC references, minors' names, and residential areas — to a public GitHub repository. That is a direct violation of the assignment's prohibition on committing sensitive data, and it is unacceptable independently of the assignment. The database must live outside the repository regardless of the technical question.
The staged decision — SQLite in development, Supabase in production
Development begins against a local SQLite file, because it requires no account, no network and no provisioning, and because the schema is reshaped hourly in the first days of the build when a hosted instance would only slow that churn down. Nothing in §6 depends on the database being remote. At the point of deployment to Netlify the same schema is migrated to a hosted Supabase Postgres instance, and the deployed application points at it through a single environment variable. Supabase is selected for production because the project already depends on Supabase Storage for CNIC images, academic documents and volunteer CVs (§10.1, FR-33.4): consolidating the database into the same project means one account, one dashboard and one set of credentials rather than two providers. The free tier — on the order of 500 MB of database and 1 GB of file storage at the time of writing, though providers revise these figures and the current allowance should be confirmed at signup — exceeds this project's requirements comfortably, and the built-in connection pooler suits a serverless host that opens database connections unpredictably.
// server/db.js — the only file aware of the environment difference

  process.env.TURSO_URL

10.4 Satisfying the Android requirement
The project brief calls for web and Android delivery. Building two native codebases is not achievable within the timeline alongside a five-component AI layer, and attempting it would compromise both.
The resolution is a single installable Progressive Web App with a manifest, icon set, service worker and offline shell. On Android it installs to the home screen, launches full-screen with a splash screen, and is indistinguishable from a native application in normal use. One codebase, one deployment, both platforms served. This is documented in the README as a deliberate architectural decision with its rationale, rather than presented as a substitution.

This section is a required chapter of the project README, not an appendix to it. The platform matches adults to children in private homes and records financial agreements; the safety design is a primary deliverable.

On SEC-19 to SEC-21
These three controls exist because the platform's primary use case sends a woman alone to an address she has not seen, arranged through an application. Most platforms in this category treat safety as a property owed to the paying customer. On Ustaad.com the tutor is equally exposed and is treated accordingly: she sets the conditions, the system enforces them rather than advertising them, and she is not penalised in her public statistics for holding to them.

18. Closing Note on Scope
This specification contains 219 functional requirements across 32 modules. That represents a realistic eleven to twelve day build for one person working full days.
The phase gating in §14 exists precisely for this reason. Phases 1 to 4 produce a complete, deployed, AI-powered application that satisfies every requirement in the project brief, including the platform's primary restricted-mobility use case, platform-owned verification, single-session engagements, payment transparency and group matching. Everything in Phase 5 raises the originality and reporting marks, but none of it is required to score well, and all of it can be surrendered without weakening a single claim made in the README.

The governing rule
A feature is worth shipping only if it works completely, appears in the demonstration path, and can be described in one line in the README. Anything that fails those three tests costs marks under completion regardless of how good the idea is. Moving an unfinished feature into §4.3 as planned but not built is a stronger outcome than shipping it half-working — it reads as product judgment rather than as a gap.
